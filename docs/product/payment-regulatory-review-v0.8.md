## Wallet assessment
Removing cash-origin refund credits, top-ups, spendable earnings, P2P and withdrawal accounts materially reduces account issuance and e-money issuance exposure. Ordinary original-tender refunds, transaction-specific obligations and settlement history are not automatically e-money wallets. Do not infer overall licence exemption. PSA s2 e-money definition includes currency-denominated/pegged value paid in advance, accepted by another person and a claim on issuer. PSA First Schedule Part 2 para 2(j) excludes services solely in respect of limited-purpose e-money; Part 3 defines limited network narrowly and monetary loyalty branch includes purchase/use issuance condition. Free registration/referral promotions are best designed as non-purchasable platform-funded checkout discounts, not paid-in stored value; do not assume statutory loyalty exclusion merely from label or lack of withdrawal. Unrelated travellers are not automatically a statutory limited network. Referrer award earned from another person's purchase and sign-up grant require assessment rather than relying on purchase-earned loyalty carve-out. Full stacking remains founder direction; use platform funds to finance seller payout, no cash conversion on cancellation, self-trade or collusion. Keep referrals disabled pending approved policy.

## Remaining payment perimeter
PSA s5 requires licence or exemption if business supplies regulated service, even incidental to main business. First Schedule Part 2 para 2(h) excludes qualifying technical support that never possesses money. Merchant acquisition can apply without possession; domestic transfer and account operation also require analysis. Merchant definition excludes individuals not required to register under Business Names Registration Act; provider onboarding and actual role of travellers, including business sellers, must be assessed. Commercial-agent exclusion para 2(a) requires genuine authorisation to negotiate/conclude sale on behalf of payer or payee, not just naming platform an agent. SGD alone does not determine domestic vs cross-border transfer. Do not assume provider licence extends to platform.

Proposed flow: shopper pays licensed provider in order-specific marketplace arrangement; provider legally controls funds and contractual payment service, validates payout/refund instructions, settles to traveller and pays earned platform commission. GetViaMe supplies order/evidence/status data and records mirrored immutable ledger; no pooled operating bank account, omnibus unrestricted platform-owned customer funds balance, arbitrary transfers, or spendable shopper/traveller balance. Obtain written provider and SG payments counsel sign-off on contracting entities, actual control/beneficial entitlement, payment agent roles, insolvency protection, AML/KYC, original-method refunds including late cases, holding and dispute release. Airwallex and Stripe MAS MPI listings include merchant acquisition and domestic transfer; Airwallex manual release documentation is candidate capability not end-to-end approval. Provider to confirm 44-day ordinary hold plus extensions and weekly payout; rematching permitted only within original request, with consent, prompt refund on withdrawal or provider/legal deadline; no reset of original collection clock and no general credits. If rematch not cleared use refund and fresh collection. Future card auth deferred; does not eliminate settlement analysis.

Risk assessment (qualitative, not regulator rating): customer-money wallet now largely designed out; promotions low subject to actual terms; transaction history low; provider-held original order settlement material until cleared; extended rematch elevated until cleared. Operating without licence is a realistic design objective, not verified conclusion. Small pilot thresholds distinguish SPI/MPI, not general exemption. MAS minimum base capital SPI SGD100k/MPI SGD250k reinforces avoiding own payment-service business if feasible.
